A verdict is the one-word answer at the top of a Chart Analyzer, Lens, or Earnings result, with a confidence percentage under it. Here is what each means, and how both get misread.

<Frame caption="Three checks, in this order. The one that surprises people is the last branch: NO TRADE comes back with its prices removed, WAIT keeps every one of them.">
  <img src="/images/diagrams/verdict-decision.svg" alt="Three checks in sequence. No directional edge gives NO TRADE, returned with no entry, stop, target or game plan. An edge price has not reached yet gives WAIT, shown on the card as HOLD, which keeps all its levels. An edge price has reached gives BUY or SELL. Confidence appears on all four and scores agreement in the evidence, not the odds of a payout." noZoom />
</Frame>

## The four chart verdicts

[Chart Analyzer](/research/chart-analyzer) returns one of four:

| Verdict | What Tradion is saying |
| --- | --- |
| **BUY** | A bullish setup, price at or near the entry, reward worth the risk. |
| **SELL** | The same in reverse: a bearish setup, price at or near the entry. |
| **WAIT** | A real setup exists, price has not reached the entry level yet. The card labels this **HOLD**. |
| **NO TRADE** | No clear directional edge at all. Unlike WAIT, there is nothing here to wait for. |

WAIT and NO TRADE are common, and neither is a failure. Not every chart holds a trade.

The difference shows up on the card. **NO TRADE** arrives with no entry, no stop, no target and no game plan, stripped so nothing reads as actionable. **WAIT** keeps all of it, because the plan is real and only the trigger is missing. Those are the levels to put an [automation](/automations/first-automation) on.

<Note>
  The tooltip on a HOLD card reads *no clear directional edge right now*, which is nearer to NO TRADE's meaning than to WAIT's. The levels are the reliable tell: a WAIT card carries an entry, a stop and a target, a NO TRADE card carries none.
</Note>

## BULLISH, BEARISH, NEUTRAL

[Lens](/research/lens) and [Earnings Spider](/research/earnings-spider) use a three-point scale instead. Chart Analyzer answers "is there a trade here" and speaks in actions; these two answer "which way does this lean". Lens labels its number **Probability**, read it as strength of the read, not odds of a payout.

When the two disagree, that is usually not a contradiction. NO TRADE on the chart with BULLISH underneath it reads as *the direction leans up, this chart does not offer a way in*.

## What confidence measures

<Frame caption="Two cards can both read 70% and mean completely different things.">
  <img src="/images/diagrams/confidence-anatomy.svg" alt="A confidence score broken into five weighted factors, each with its own score and reason" noZoom />
</Frame>

On a Chart Analyzer card, confidence is a weighted average of five factors, each scored 0–100:

| Factor | Weight | What it scores |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trend clarity | 30% | How cleanly price is moving one way. Choppy sideways action scores low. |
| Pattern quality | 25% | How well-defined the formation is, a clean flag beats a vague one. |
| Volume | 20% | Whether volume (shares changing hands) backs the move. A breakout on thin volume scores low. |
| Risk/reward | 15% | What you stand to gain against what you stand to lose. |
| Market context | 10% | Sector behaviour and conditions around the ticker. |

Each factor comes with a one-line reason. **Read the factors, not the headline number.** A 70% built from clarity 95 and volume 20 is a clean pattern nobody is trading. A 70% built from five scores near 70 is a middling setup. Not the same trade.

<Note>
  Only Chart Analyzer works this way. Lens gives you one number and no breakdown. Earnings Spider shows a breakdown, but its overall figure is the model's own judgement rather than arithmetic on the factors under it. Same 0–100 scale in all three; different amounts of showing the working.
</Note>

### What confidence is not

<Warning>
  **Confidence is not the probability that the trade makes money.** A verdict at 80% does not win 8 times in 10. It means the evidence pointed one direction more consistently than on a 40% setup.
</Warning>

It ignores your position size, your holding period, and news that breaks tomorrow. The separate **Setup Quality** figure, out of 10, averages trend clarity and pattern quality only: it describes the chart, while confidence describes the whole call.

## Entry, stop, target, and the ratio between them

<Frame caption="Left, the geometry: risk is entry to stop, reward is entry to target, and a stop inside the ATR band is taken out by ordinary movement. Right, what each ratio costs you, expressed as the win rate you need to break even.">
  <img src="/images/diagrams/trade-levels.svg" alt="A price axis with target above, entry in the middle and stop below, a dashed band one ATR either side of entry marked as ordinary movement, and the spans labelled reward and risk. Beside it, four ratios with the break-even win rate each needs: 3:1 needs 25%, 2:1 needs 33%, 1.5:1 needs 40%, 1:1 needs 50%, with a line marking where Tradion prints the ratio in red" noZoom />
</Frame>

A chart verdict comes with three prices: **entry**, where the setup makes sense; **stop**, where you exit if it goes against you; **target**, where the move is expected to run out.

**Risk/reward** is the distance from entry to target divided by the distance from entry to stop, written like `3:1`. At 3:1 you gain three times what you risk, so you can be right well under half the time and still come out ahead. Tradion flags anything below **1.5:1** in red.

That red flag is a flat 1.5 whatever mode you selected, but the analysis was held to a different bar: **2:1 for swing, 1.5:1 for day trades, 1:1 for scalps**. A swing setup at 1.8:1 therefore prints in ordinary colour while sitting under the standard it was graded against. Check the ratio against your mode, not against the colour.

The ratio is arithmetic on those three prices, not a separate judgement. Move the stop further away and the ratio falls, which is how a good-looking setup quietly becomes mediocre.

<Note>
  A card titled *Conditional Levels*, marked "not actionable, waiting for trigger", is the WAIT case above. The ratio shown is what you would get at that entry, not from here.
</Note>

## The levels above and below the price

Chart Analyzer also returns three reference prices for a stated time window:

- **Pivot point**, a neutral reference: the previous session's high, low, and close averaged.
- **R1 (resistance)**, a price above, where selling has tended to appear and the move stalls.
- **S1 (support)**, a price below, where buying has tended to appear and the fall stops.

R1 and S1 each carry a **break probability**: the estimated chance price pushes all the way through inside that window. The model reads it off the shape of the chart, not from counting past occurrences, so treat 55% and 45% as "roughly a coin flip either way".

The set has to survive a check before you see it: R1 above the current price, S1 below it, each at least half an ATR away. Fail any of that and the whole block is dropped rather than patched, so **a card with no pivot levels is not an omission**. The numbers did not hold together.

The card also shows **ATR** (Average True Range), how far a symbol typically travels in one period, in dollars. If your stop is tighter than the ATR, ordinary movement takes you out before the idea gets a chance. On a chart card the ATR is read off the picture you uploaded, an estimate resting on an estimate. Lens calculates its own from real price bars, so trust that one when they differ.

## In plain English

Read the card in this order:

1. **The word**, a reading of the chart, not an instruction.
2. **The five factors**, not the headline percentage. Two very different setups can average to the same number.
3. **The three prices.** If entry-to-stop is smaller than the ATR, the stop is too tight for this symbol.
4. **The ratio.** Anything red needs you to be right most of the time.

Nothing on the card knows your account size, your other positions, or what you will do once you are in.

## When to distrust a verdict

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The chart image is hard to read">
    Chart Analyzer and Lens read pixels. Heavy indicator overlays, a cropped price axis, or a low-resolution capture all degrade the read. If the analysis describes levels not on your chart, recapture it cleanly.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The factor scores don't line up">
    Low volume means a pattern nobody is trading, and those resolve badly. A wide spread across the five scores means conflicting evidence. A tight cluster, high or low, is a more reliable read than an average of extremes.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A major event is pending">
    Earnings, an economic release, or a rate decision can override chart structure entirely. Technical analysis two days before earnings is analysis of a chart, not of the situation.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="You expected it to know your history">
    Chart Analyzer does not. The verdict comes from the image and nothing else; your optional notes are read as context and cannot move the call, by design. Lens is the personalised one, drawing on [Tradion Memory](/concepts/tradion-memory), so with few autopsies logged that layer has little to say and its read is closer to generic.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Nobody is keeping score

Tradion saves every verdict it gives you and never goes back to check it. There is no accuracy figure on a past call, no win rate by verdict, no record of where price went afterwards. That is the second reason not to read confidence as odds: no number on the card has ever been measured against an outcome.

So keep the score yourself. [Log the trade](/trade-intelligence/logging-a-trade) you took off a verdict and let [Trade Autopsy](/trade-intelligence/trade-autopsy) grade how you handled it. Ten of those tell you the thing no card can: whether these reads work on the instruments and timeframes you actually trade.

## Verdicts are analysis, not advice

A verdict is one input from a model reading a chart, and it can be wrong in ways the confidence score gives no warning about. The decision is yours every time.
